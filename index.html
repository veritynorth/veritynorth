Benjamin Franklin Was America’s First Influencer

France, 1776–1785

Before America looked permanent, powerful, or even especially likely to survive, Benjamin Franklin arrived in France with a fur cap, a reputation, and an almost dangerous understanding of public attention. He did not simply become famous in France. He made fame politically useful.

Through Franklin, America began to look less like a distant rebellion and more like a cause France could support.

About the Author

Meet Verity North

Verity North writes history for readers who like their nonfiction sharp, readable, and allergic to marble-statue worship. Her work explores public image, private ambition, spectacle, strategy, and the carefully managed chaos behind famous historical figures.

She believes history gets much more interesting once people stop pretending everyone involved was either a saint, a villain, or a powdered-wigged cardboard cutout.
